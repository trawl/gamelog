# Pocha — quick reference

*Pocha (also called "la podrida") is a traditional Spanish card game with no
official publisher rule book, and rules vary by region and family. This sheet
keeps to what several rule descriptions agree on and to what the app scores.
Agree the variants below before you start. 3–6 players.*

## Goal

Before each hand, every player predicts exactly how many tricks they will win.
You score by getting it **exactly right**, not by winning the most tricks.
**Highest total after the last hand wins.**

## Deck and trump

- **Spanish 40 or 48 card deck.** Card order, high to low: ace, 3, king, knight
  (caballo), jack (sota), 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 2. Cards carry no points.
- Deal one card at a time. The next card is turned face up and its suit is
  **trump** (*triunfo*). Any trump beats any card of another suit.
- With 3 players the 2s are usually removed. At most 5 players can take part in
  the 8-card hands (8 x 5 = 40, the whole 40-card deck), and up to 6 if using
  the 48-card deck.

## Hands

The number of cards dealt changes every hand. The app uses 18 hands:

| Hands | Cards each |
|---|---|
| 1 to 7 | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 (going up) |
| 8 to 11 | 8 cards, four hands in a row |
| 12 to 18 | 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 (going down) |


## Bidding

- Starting with the player who leads (to the dealer's right) and ending with
  the dealer, everyone says how many tricks they expect to win.
- **Last bidder restriction:** the last player to bid may **not** choose a number
  that makes the total of all bids equal the number of tricks in the hand. At
  least one player must therefore miss.

## Playing

- The player to the dealer's right leads any card.
- You must **follow suit** if you can. If you cannot, you must play a **trump**
  if you have one. If you have neither, play anything.
- Sources disagree on whether you must also **beat** the winning card when you
  can (a higher card of the led suit, or a higher trump). Agree this first.
- If a trump is led, everyone holding a trump must play one.
- The highest trump wins the trick, or, if there is none, the highest card of
  the led suit. The winner leads the next trick.

## Scoring

The commonly published scoring, which the app uses, is **10 points plus 5 per
trick won** for an exact bid, and **-5 per trick of difference** (over or
under) for a miss.

| Result | Points |
|---|---|
| Exact bid | 10 + 5 per trick won |
| Missed bid | -5 per trick over or under |

Example: bid 3 and win 3 = 25 points. Bid 3 and win 1 = -10.

A bid of zero that is kept scores the flat 10. Some tables play with other
values, so agree on them before you start.

## Ties and other variants

- If the final totals are tied, common rules say to deal extra one-card hands
  until it is broken, or to prefer the player with fewer missed bids. The app
  simply **shares the victory** between tied players.
- Variants you may meet, not scored by the app: a "Pocha" call worth 100 points
  with 5 or more players, hands with the trump chosen by auction, hands without
  trump, hands where you see only the others' cards, and score multipliers for
  certain trump suits.
- Penalties for playing a wrong card (*renuncio*) are usually agreed in advance.

## Gamelog tips

- The board counts the hands and shows how many cards to deal. The four 8-card
  hands are labelled with one suit each, using the suit names of the chosen
  deck (Spanish: coins, cups, swords, clubs). The other hands are labelled
  "going up" or "going down".
- For each player, enter the bid and the tricks won. The tricks won must add up
  to the cards dealt in that hand.
- While entering bids, the app **blocks the last bid that would make the total
  equal the cards dealt**, for whichever player is the last one without a bid, so
  enter the bids in playing order with the dealer last.
- The score of each hand is worked out for you from the bid and the tricks won,
  as in the table above.
